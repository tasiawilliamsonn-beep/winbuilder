The W.I.N. Cycle

What I Need: every student gets reteaching, practice, or extension, every day.

Grades 5 and 6 math and ELA · Indiana Academic Standards (2023) · aligned to ILEARN

Talking pointsUse this guide to launch W.I.N. with a team or a new teacher. Every slide ends with notes like these.1

Why W.I.N.

What W.I.N. time is

  • A protected block where students work on the standards they need, not the next lesson in the pacing guide.
  • Groups come from data, and they change as students grow. No one is stuck in a group.
  • Every day has three levels of practice: Below, At, and Above grade level.
  • Practice mirrors ILEARN: about half open-ended, with students showing work and explaining their thinking.
  • Every unit ends with a 12-question open-ended assessment that shows who mastered each standard.
Talking pointsW.I.N. is not a second math or ELA class. It's targeted time: reteach, practice, or stretch, based on evidence.2

The big picture

The W.I.N. cycle

  1. Assess: yesterday's work, the score sheet, or the dashboard.
  2. Group: sort students into Below, At, and Above.
  3. Teach: the small group lesson, starting with Below.
  4. Practice: each student works at their level, showing work.
  5. Check: score with the key, note confidence, and regroup.
Talking pointsThe cycle turns every day. Today's check is tomorrow's assessment, so groups stay current.3

Differentiation

Three levels, every day

Below · Level 1

For students rebuilding the skill.
  • Friendlier numbers
  • Fewer answer choices
  • Supports: hints, a worked example, sentence starters
  • Small group first

At · Level 2

On grade level, like ILEARN.
  • Typed answers and multi-part items
  • About half open-ended
  • Show work on every problem
  • Explain how you know

Above · Level 3

Extension: students produce.
  • Find and fix errors
  • Write and solve their own problems
  • Solve two ways and justify
  • Rewrite, argue, create
Talking pointsStudents see "Level 1, 2, 3." Keep "below" and "above" for teacher materials.5

Teaching

The small group lesson

1. Big ideaSay it in one sentence. Students say it back.
2. NotesStudents fill in the blanks as you talk.
3. Watch meModel one step at a time. Students copy the steps.
4. Try it with meStudents try first, then check together.
5. Stop and jotStudents explain in their own words.
  • The lesson plan has every blank filled in and the mistakes to watch for.
  • Students use the Small group notes page (paper) or the portal.
  • Keep it to 10 to 15 minutes. Send students to practice with the target fresh.
Talking pointsI do, we do, you do. The jot tells you who is ready for Level 2.7

Rigor

Open-ended practice, like ILEARN

About half of every set is open-ended
  • Typed numeric answers, not just choices
  • Estimates with rounding steps
  • Two-part questions: solve, then explain
  • ELA short responses with two text details
Every answer shows thinking
  • A "Show your work" box on every math problem
  • "How I know" lines under ELA choices
  • Written responses are scored 0 to 2 with a guide
  • Answer keys name the misconception behind each wrong answer
Talking pointsILEARN mixes selected and constructed responses. Practicing both builds stamina and shows you why students miss.8

Access

Needs supports

What the supports are
  • A hint printed with each problem
  • A "Remember" box with the notes and a worked example
  • Sentence starters for written answers
  • One fewer answer choice
Who gets them
  • Level 1 worksheets by default
  • Any student with IEP, 504, or EL accommodations
  • Students turn them on in the portal with "I need supports"
  • Fade supports as accuracy rises
Talking pointsIn the packet, choose supports for Level 1 only, every level, or none. The answer keys adjust automatically.10

Data

Check and regroup

Below

  • Small group reteach
  • Level 1 with supports

At

60 to 84% correct.
  • Level 2 practice
  • Small group as needed

Above

85% or more and confidence 3 to 4.
  • Level 3 extension
  • IXL or RACE

Canvas: drop the submissions zip on the dashboard.   Paper: mark with the key and fill in the class score sheet.

Talking pointsGroups are fluid. Move students every day the data says so, and watch for high confidence with low accuracy.11

Closing the cycle

The end-of-unit assessment

  • 12 open-ended questions across every standard in the unit, however many days you planned.
  • Math: show-your-work boxes and two-part questions. ELA: a new passage and written responses.
  • Typed answers are 1 point. Written and two-part answers are 2 points, scored with the guide.
  • The key includes a mastery by standard table: 80% of a standard's points means mastered.
  • Standards below 80% become the next W.I.N. unit.
